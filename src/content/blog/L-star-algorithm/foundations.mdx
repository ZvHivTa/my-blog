## The target is stronger than a minimal DFA

Let $L\subseteq\Sigma^*$ be a regular language. Two words are syntactically congruent when no left or right context can distinguish them:

$$
u\sim_L v
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall x,y\in\Sigma^*,\;xuy\in L\iff xvy\in L.
$$

This is a congruence with respect to concatenation. If $[u]$ denotes the equivalence class of $u$, multiplication is defined by

$$
[u]\cdot[v]=[uv].
$$

The quotient

$$
M(L)=\Sigma^*/{\sim_L}
$$

is the **syntactic monoid** of $L$. It is the smallest monoid recognizing $L$ and is isomorphic to the transition monoid of the minimal DFA for $L$.

Classical L-star learning distinguishes words by their future behavior. That is enough to identify minimal-DFA states, but syntactic congruence also quantifies over prefixes placed before a word. The extended learner must therefore collect both right experiments and left contexts.

## Query access

The learner does not inspect the target language directly. It communicates with a teacher through two interfaces.

### Membership query

For a chosen word $w\in\Sigma^*$, the teacher returns its characteristic value

$$
\chi_L(w)=
\begin{cases}
1,&w\in L,\\
0,&w\notin L.
\end{cases}
$$

In the implementation, a membership query is abbreviated as `MQ(w)`.

### Equivalence query

The learner submits a hypothesis DFA $\mathcal H$. An exact teacher answers whether

$$
L(\mathcal H)=L.
$$

If the languages differ, the teacher returns a counterexample in their symmetric difference. The current experimental Oracle knows the target DFA and uses breadth-first search to return a shortest counterexample.

This exact EQ model is useful for validating the algorithm. It is not assumed to be a realistic interface for every black-box system; the distinction becomes important in the experimental cost model.

## The observation structure

The learner maintains three finite sets:

- $I\subseteq\Sigma^*$: current representative words;
- $P\subseteq\Sigma^*$: known left contexts;
- $S\subseteq\Sigma^*$: known suffix experiments.

All three initially contain only $\epsilon$. The one-letter right boundary of the representative set is

$$
I\Sigma=\{ia\mid i\in I,\;a\in\Sigma\}.
$$

The finite observation function is

$$
T(p,i,s)=\chi_L(pis),
$$

where $p\in P$, $i\in I\cup I\Sigma$ and $s\in S$. This can be viewed as a three-dimensional table, although the current implementation also keeps a global query cache keyed by the complete word $pis$.

## Full rows and suffix rows

For any word $w$, its full row is the vector

$$
\operatorname{row}_{P,S}(w)
=
\bigl(\chi_L(pws)\bigr)_{(p,s)\in P\times S}.
$$

Two words are **status-equivalent** under the current finite observation when

$$
u\equiv_{P,S}v
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{row}_{P,S}(u)=\operatorname{row}_{P,S}(v).
$$

This is only a finite approximation to syntactic congruence. Enlarging $P$ or $S$ can split a class that looked equal before.

Right consistency uses a second, deliberately narrower row:

$$
\operatorname{row}_{S}(w)
=
\bigl(\chi_L(ws)\bigr)_{s\in S}.
$$

Keeping these notions separate matters. Full rows represent the current two-sided quotient, while suffix rows maintain the ordinary right behavior required for a well-formed hypothesis DFA.

## From rows to a hypothesis

Once every boundary word in $I\Sigma$ has a representative with the same full row, the distinct rows of $I$ become hypothesis states:

$$
Q=\{\operatorname{row}_{P,S}(i)\mid i\in I\}.
$$

The initial state is the row of $\epsilon$. A state is accepting when its representative belongs to $L$. For $a\in\Sigma$, its transition is determined by the row of the representative followed by $a$:

$$
\delta\bigl(\operatorname{row}_{P,S}(i),a\bigr)
=\operatorname{row}_{P,S}(ia).
$$

Closedness guarantees that this target row is represented inside $I$. Right consistency ensures that choosing equivalent representatives does not change the transition behavior.
