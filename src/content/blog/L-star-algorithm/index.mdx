## Research goal

Angluin's L-star algorithm learns the minimal DFA of an unknown regular language through membership queries and equivalence queries. This project asks a stronger question:

> Can an observation-table learner recover the syntactic monoid directly, while keeping the number and cost of external queries under control?

For a regular language $L\subseteq\Sigma^*$, the minimal DFA captures right-language equivalence. The syntactic monoid instead identifies two words only when they behave identically in every two-sided context:

$$
u\sim_L v
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall x,y\in\Sigma^*,\;xuy\in L\iff xvy\in L.
$$

The learner therefore has to preserve ordinary DFA behavior and refine enough left contexts to recover the stronger algebraic quotient.

## Current system

The current prototype is a runnable active-learning system with:

- a three-dimensional observation structure indexed by representatives, left contexts and suffixes;
- closedness and right-consistency refinement;
- exact equivalence queries against a target DFA;
- a post-EQ Monoid Consistency Check;
- two competing integration strategies, `post_check` and `promote_to_p`;
- global membership-query caching and local row caches;
- reproducible benchmarks for Commutative and Star-free languages.

The central algorithmic choice is when to add a discovered representative to the set of left contexts. `post_check` adds such contexts lazily when an algebraic inconsistency is observed. `promote_to_p` adds them eagerly, trading a larger observation structure for fewer equivalence queries.

## Current result

The fixed experiment from August 25, 2026 contains 480 classified cases. Across those cases, Promote reduced the number of equivalence queries from 1,725 to 1,014, a reduction of 41.2%. This came with a 5.13-fold increase in materialized cells and about 17% more aggregated local median time.

This does not establish one universally superior strategy. It gives a decision rule: Promote becomes attractive when an external equivalence query is expensive enough to compensate for its additional local and memory cost.

## What is established and what remains open

The exact-oracle prototype, both strategies, the cache architecture and the August experiment are implemented. The latest milestone is reproducible from saved JSON data and tagged source history.

The following items remain future work:

- removing the redundant materialized three-dimensional `table` from the main learner;
- recording membership-query length and timing distributions;
- replaying query traces under realistic MQ and EQ cost models;
- adding random or PAC-style equivalence queries for unknown black boxes;
- extending the benchmark toward larger and more difficult Commutative languages.

For a non-regular target, the intended output would no longer be an exact syntactic monoid. It would be a finite quotient observed under a stated distribution, confidence level and query budget.
