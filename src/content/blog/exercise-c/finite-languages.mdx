## Conclusion

Let $L\subseteq\Sigma^+$ be finite, and let $S=(S_f,S_\omega)$ be the Wilke algebra constructed from its syntactic semigroup. Then

$$
|S_\omega|=1.
$$

The proof connects the shape of the minimal DFA of a finite language to the right-zero criterion.

## The unique dead state

Let

$$
\mathcal A=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_0,F)
$$

be the minimal DFA of $L$. Because $L$ is finite, sufficiently long words cannot be accepted. The minimal DFA therefore contains a dead state $q_d$ satisfying

$$
\delta(q_d,a)=q_d
\qquad\text{for every }a\in\Sigma.
$$

This dead state is unique. Two distinct dead states would have the same empty right language and would be merged by minimization.

Every other state is co-accessible: from it, some continuation reaches a final state. Otherwise that state would also have an empty right language and would coincide with $q_d$.

## Idempotent transformations collapse to the dead state

Take an idempotent element $e=f_w\in S_f$, so $f_w^2=f_w$. For every state $q\in Q$, the image $f_w(q)$ is a fixed point of $f_w$:

$$
f_w(f_w(q))=f_w(q).
$$

Suppose some image $r=f_w(q)$ were not the dead state. Since $r$ is co-accessible, there would be a word $u$ taking $r$ to a final state. But $r$ is fixed by $w$, so all words

$$
w^n u \qquad (n\ge 1)
$$

would lead from $q$ to acceptance. Because $q$ is reachable in a minimal DFA, prefixing a word that reaches $q$ would produce infinitely many words in $L$, contradicting finiteness.

Hence every idempotent transformation maps all states to $q_d$. It is therefore the zero transformation $0$, satisfying

$$
s0=0s=0
$$

for every $s\in S_f$. In particular, every idempotent is right-zero.

## Apply the criterion

The right-zero theorem now gives

$$
|S_\omega|=1.
$$

This explains the finite example $L=\{a,ab\}$ without requiring a separate conjugacy computation, and it extends the same conclusion to every finite regular language.
