Let $\Sigma=\{a,b\}$. We work through one finite language and one infinite regular language. Although their finite semigroups differ, both yield a one-element infinite sort.

## Example 1: $L=\{a,ab\}$

### Minimal DFA

Let $Q=\{q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3\}$, $F=\{q_1,q_2\}$, and let $q_3$ be the dead state. The transition function is

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & a & b\\ \hline
q_0 & q_1 & q_3\\
q_1 & q_3 & q_2\\
q_2 & q_3 & q_3\\
q_3 & q_3 & q_3
\end{array}
$$

### Syntactic semigroup

The distinct nonempty transformations are represented by

$$
S_f=\{a,b,aa,ab\},
$$

with relations including

$$
ba=bb=aa,\qquad aab=aaa=aa.
$$

The only idempotent is $aa$. Checking $se=s$ shows that the linked-pair set is

$$
\mathcal L(S_f)=\{(aa,aa)\}.
$$

Therefore

$$
S_\omega=\{[aa,aa]\}.
$$

A common idempotent exponent is $n=2$, so

$$
\pi(a)=[aa,aa],\qquad
\pi(b)=[bb,bb]=[aa,aa].
$$

Selecting the unique class gives $U=\Sigma^\omega$.

## Example 2: $L=\Sigma^*ab$

### Minimal DFA

Let $Q=\{q_0,q_1,q_2\}$, $F=\{q_2\}$, with transition table

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & a & b\\ \hline
q_0 & q_1 & q_0\\
q_1 & q_1 & q_2\\
q_2 & q_1 & q_0
\end{array}
$$

### Syntactic semigroup

The distinct transformations are

$$
S_f=\{a,b,ab,bb\},
$$

with Cayley table

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 & a & b & ab & bb\\ \hline
a  & a & ab & ab & bb\\
b  & a & bb & ab & bb\\
ab & a & bb & ab & bb\\
bb & a & bb & ab & bb
\end{array}
$$

The idempotents are

$$
E(S_f)=\{a,ab,bb\}.
$$

They are right-zero elements, so the linked pairs are

$$
\mathcal L(S_f)=\{(a,a),(ab,ab),(bb,bb)\}.
$$

These linked pairs are conjugate. For example, for $(a,a)\sim(ab,ab)$, choose $x=ab$ and $y=a$; for $(a,a)\sim(bb,bb)$, choose $x=bb$ and $y=a$. Hence

$$
[a,a]=[ab,ab]=[bb,bb]
$$

and

$$
S_\omega=\{[a,a]\}.
$$

Again the common exponent $n=2$ works, and selecting the unique class yields $U=\Sigma^\omega$.

## What the examples reveal

The two finite sorts have different sizes and multiplication rules. Their shared feature is not the shape of the DFA or the number of idempotents: it is that every idempotent is right-zero. This observation motivates the theorem in the next article.
