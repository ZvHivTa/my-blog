The proven results use a semigroup condition: every idempotent is right-zero. A natural next question is whether a purely graph-theoretic property of the minimal DFA can also force ω-triviality.

## Conjecture from the report

Let

$$
\mathcal A=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_0,F)
$$

be the minimal DFA of $L$. If all states belong to a single strongly connected component, then the associated Wilke algebra $S=(S_f,S_\omega)$ satisfies

$$
|S_\omega|=1.
$$

This statement is recorded as a **conjecture**, not as a consequence of the right-zero theorem.

## Why the previous proof does not apply directly

For a finite or definite language, idempotent transformations collapse behavior toward a terminal suffix or dead state. A strongly connected DFA has the opposite geometry: every state can return to every other state, so there is generally no dead state and idempotents need not be right-zero.

Consequently, proving the conjecture would require a different argument. One would need to show directly that all linked pairs are conjugate, even when their idempotent components are not right-zero.

## A possible investigation strategy

1. Generate small minimal strongly connected DFAs over a fixed alphabet.
2. Compute each transition semigroup and its idempotents.
3. Enumerate the linked pairs $(s,e)$ with $se=s$.
4. Compute conjugacy classes and record $|S_\omega|$.
5. Search for either a counterexample or an invariant explaining why only one class occurs.

Useful intermediate questions include:

- Does transitivity of the DFA action imply transitivity on linked pairs?
- Which properties of the transition semigroup replace the right-zero argument?
- Is one SCC sufficient, or is a stronger condition such as synchronizing behavior required?

## Current status

The exercise establishes the finite and definite cases, but leaves the SCC statement open. It should therefore be presented as a research direction until a proof or counterexample is obtained.
