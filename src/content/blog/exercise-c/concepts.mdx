## From a minimal DFA to a syntactic semigroup

Let

$$
\mathcal A=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_0,F)
$$

be the minimal DFA of a regular language $L$. Every word $w\in\Sigma^+$ induces a transformation

$$
f_w:Q\to Q,\qquad f_w(q)=\delta^*(q,w).
$$

Composition of these transformations corresponds to concatenation of words. The set of distinct transformations induced by nonempty words is the **transition semigroup** of the minimal DFA. It is isomorphic to the syntactic semigroup $S_L$, the smallest finite semigroup that recognizes $L$.

A practical computation is:

1. write the transformations induced by the generators $a\in\Sigma$ as vectors;
2. close this set under composition, often with a Cayley table;
3. identify words that induce the same transformation;
4. record the multiplication table of the distinct transformations.

The identity transformation $f_\varepsilon$ is excluded from a semigroup generated by $\Sigma^+$. Adding it produces the corresponding transition monoid.

## Idempotents and right-zero elements

An element $e\in S$ is **idempotent** when $e^2=e$. An element $e$ is **right-zero** when

$$
se=e \qquad \text{for every }s\in S.
$$

These notions are crucial because linked pairs are built from idempotents, while the main theorem identifies right-zero idempotents as a sufficient condition for ω-triviality.

## Wilke algebra

A Wilke algebra is a two-sorted algebra

$$
S=(S_f,S_\omega)
$$

with three operations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\odot_f &: S_f\times S_f\to S_f,\\
\odot_{\mathrm{mix}} &: S_f\times S_\omega\to S_\omega,\\
\pi &: S_f\to S_\omega.
\end{aligned}
$$

The finite multiplication is associative, the mixed product is compatible with it, and the ω-power satisfies the characteristic identities

$$
\begin{aligned}
s\odot_{\mathrm{mix}}\pi(t\odot_f s)&=\pi(s\odot_f t),\\
\pi(s^n)&=\pi(s) \qquad (n\ge 1).
\end{aligned}
$$

The first identity expresses the rotation law $(st)^\omega=s(ts)^\omega$; the second says that repeating a finite block before taking its ω-power does not change the resulting infinite behavior.

## Completeness

A Wilke algebra is **complete** when every $u\in S_\omega$ can be represented as an ultimately periodic product:

$$
u=s\odot_{\mathrm{mix}}\pi(t)
$$

for some $s,t\in S_f$. All Wilke algebras considered in this exercise are complete.
