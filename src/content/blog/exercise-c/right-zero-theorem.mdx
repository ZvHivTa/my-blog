Let $S=(S_f,S_\omega)$ be the complete Wilke algebra constructed from a finite semigroup $S_f$.

## Theorem

If every idempotent $e\in S_f$ is right-zero, then

$$
|S_\omega|=1.
$$

Equivalently, all linked pairs of $S_f$ belong to one conjugacy class.

## Step 1: determine all linked pairs

Let $E=E(S_f)$. Since every $e\in E$ is right-zero,

$$
se=e
$$

for every $s\in S_f$. A linked pair must also satisfy $se=s$. Combining the two equations gives $s=e$. Therefore

$$
\mathcal L(S_f)=\{(e,e)\mid e\in E\}.
$$

No linked pairs with different first and second components can occur.

## Step 2: show that any two are conjugate

Take $(e,e)$ and $(e',e')$ in $\mathcal L(S_f)$. To prove conjugacy, choose

$$
x=e',\qquad y=e.
$$

Because both $e$ and $e'$ are right-zero,

$$
\begin{aligned}
xy&=e'e=e,\\
yx&=ee'=e',\\
ex&=ee'=e'.
\end{aligned}
$$

These are exactly the equations required for

$$
(e,e)\sim(e',e').
$$

Thus every linked pair lies in the same conjugacy class, proving $|S_\omega|=1$.

## Why this theorem matters

Directly computing conjugacy classes can become expensive as the semigroup grows. The theorem replaces that quotient computation with a local test on idempotents:

1. compute $E(S_f)$;
2. verify $se=e$ for all $s\in S_f$ and $e\in E(S_f)$;
3. conclude ω-triviality immediately.

The remaining results identify language classes whose syntactic semigroups automatically pass this test.
