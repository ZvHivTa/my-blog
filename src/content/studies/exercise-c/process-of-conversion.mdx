Let $S_f$ be the syntactic semigroup of a regular language. The finite sort and its multiplication are retained. The new work is to construct the infinite sort $S_\omega$.

## Step 1: find the idempotents

Compute

$$
E(S_f)=\{e\in S_f\mid e^2=e\}.
$$

Every finite semigroup contains idempotents, and every infinite repetition eventually reaches one through a suitable power.

## Step 2: enumerate linked pairs

A pair $(s,e)\in S_f\times E(S_f)$ is a **linked pair** if

$$
se=s.
$$

Let

$$
\mathcal L(S_f)=\{(s,e)\mid e^2=e \text{ and } se=s\}.
$$

The pair represents an ultimately periodic behavior: a finite prefix represented by $s$, followed by infinite repetition of a block represented by $e$.

## Step 3: quotient by conjugacy

Two linked pairs $(s,e)$ and $(s',e')$ are conjugate, written

$$
(s,e)\sim(s',e'),
$$

if there exist $x,y\in S_f$ such that

$$
e=xy,\qquad e'=yx,\qquad s'=sx.
$$

These equations also imply $s=s'y$, because $s'y=sxy=se=s$. The infinite sort is the quotient

$$
S_\omega=\mathcal L(S_f)/{\sim}.
$$

Thus $|S_\omega|$ is exactly the number of conjugacy classes of linked pairs.

## Step 4: define the operations

For $t\in S_f$ and $[s,e]\in S_\omega$, define

$$
t\odot_{\mathrm{mix}}[s,e]=[ts,e].
$$

Because $S_f$ is finite, there is a common positive exponent $n$ such that $u^n$ is idempotent for every $u\in S_f$. Then

$$
\pi(t)=t^\omega=[t^n,t^n].
$$

The definitions are independent of the chosen representatives and give a complete Wilke algebra.

## Recognition of an ω-language

An ω-language $U\subseteq\Sigma^\omega$ is recognized by $S=(S_f,S_\omega)$ if there is a morphism $\varphi$ and an accepting set $X\subseteq S_\omega$ such that

$$
U=\varphi^{-1}(X)
=\{w\in\Sigma^\omega\mid\varphi(w)\in X\}.
$$

This yields a direct test for the property studied here: enumerate linked pairs, compute their conjugacy classes, and check whether only one class remains.
